01 · The challenge

An existing programme with room to work harder.

Saxo wanted Refer a Friend to play a stronger role in client acquisition. Internally, it was treated as a “Must Win Battle,” making visibility and participation important business priorities.

The design challenge went beyond adding more promotional placements. Clients needed to understand what they could earn, how the rewards worked and what happened after they invited someone.

Research pointed to issues with awareness and reward comprehension. Those findings helped shape a broader redesign of the experience, rather than a purely promotional update.

My contribution

From platform experience to cross-team collaboration.

I owned the product-design work for the platform referral experience, translating research into design decisions and working with stakeholders to shape the direction.

The work covered entry points, reward explanations, referral status and a shared experience across markets. For the public-facing referral journey, I collaborated with the web design team, providing UX direction and a design foundation for their work.

02 · Design decisions

More visibility didn’t have to mean more placements.

Leadership wanted the programme promoted more prominently. I mapped the platform’s most visited pages and proposed entry points where clients were likely to encounter them.

The design included a revised referral icon, a placement on the portfolio page and access through the profile card.

When leadership asked for further promotion, I explained how the proposed placements already covered high-traffic areas and questioned what additional value more placements would provide. My concern was that expanding coverage further could add clutter without a proportionate benefit.

Leadership was initially reluctant, but we reached agreement on the focused approach. This wasn’t about resisting the commercial objective. It was about finding a way to support it while protecting the wider product experience.

03 · Reward clarity

Making the incentive understandable.

The reward needed to be clear before clients could decide whether referring someone was worth doing.

Saxo’s programme used commission credits, which offset trading fees. The experience needed to explain that mechanism alongside what the referrer and their friend would receive.

I redesigned the rewards section to make those distinctions easier to understand, rather than relying on clients to interpret the terminology themselves.

Referral status also became part of the experience, giving clients visibility into whether their invitation had progressed to signup and account funding.

Making the incentive and reward mechanism easier to understand.

04 · Scaling across markets

One shared structure, with room for local differences.

The experience needed to work across 14 markets with different legal requirements and reward structures.

The aim was not to make every market identical. It was to establish a shared structure that could accommodate those differences without requiring an entirely separate experience for each market.

That meant considering local requirements as part of the design, rather than treating them as changes to apply after the main experience was finished.

Market-specific visual variations within the shared referral experience.

05 · Cross-team collaboration

Connecting the platform and public-facing journey.

A referral involves two people and more than one surface. The existing client shares an invitation through the platform, while their friend encounters Saxo through a public-facing page.

I worked with the web design team to connect those parts of the journey, providing UX direction and a design foundation they could build on.

The important distinction was continuity: the invitation, reward explanation and next step needed to make sense across the journey, even where different teams owned the implementation.

The public-facing referral journey — developed with the web design team, with my contribution focused on UX direction and the design foundation.

06 · The complete experience

Bringing the design together.

The redesigned platform experience brought the invitation, reward explanation and referral status into a dedicated experience.

The individual changes supported the same direction: make the programme easier to discover, make the incentive easier to understand and help clients see what happened after sharing an invitation.

07 · Results

Growth, with appropriate context.

The product owner subsequently reported that referrals had tripled year over year following the redesign.

That is a meaningful business outcome, but it should not be attributed to design alone. Other activity was taking place, and the referral experience was one contribution to the wider result.

Earlier reporting also indicated fewer questions about the programme and its rewards, consistent with the work to make the experience clearer.

Looking back

Balancing ambition with a clear experience.

The project required decisions at several levels: where the programme should appear, how its rewards should be explained and how a shared experience could work across different markets.

One of the most important discussions concerned promotion. A high-priority initiative naturally created pressure for more visibility. My responsibility was to explain how the proposed design supported that goal and make the trade-offs clear enough to reach agreement.

The case demonstrates the kind of product-design work I enjoy: connecting business ambition with a clear customer experience, staying close to the interface details and working across teams to make the direction achievable.
