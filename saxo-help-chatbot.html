Saxo Bank

Product Designer
Timeframe
Three weeks
Scope
Cross-platform help

Making room for a new way to get help.

Redesigning Saxo’s help experience to make its new AI chatbot easier to discover and use—while preserving the support options clients relied on.

Challenge

Saxo wanted more clients to use its upgraded chatbot, but how to achieve that across different platforms was unresolved.

Approach

Define a feasible direction through research, prototyping and close collaboration with product and engineering—using existing components without modification.

Outcome

The work contributed to approximately 70% more engaged chatbot conversations.

01 · The challenge

A clear ambition. An undefined solution.

Saxo had upgraded its AI chatbot engine and wanted clients to start using it quickly. The business objective was clear: increase adoption. The experience needed to achieve that was not.

The platforms had different layouts, existing help experiences and user needs. A consistent approach needed to work across those differences without making other support options harder to reach.

Together with the PM, I structured a three-week sprint to investigate the problem, explore a direction and take the design through implementation.

Working within what already existed

We had to use existing design-system components as they stood. New components, modifications and custom workarounds would have required additional development beyond the project’s scope. Feasibility therefore shaped the design from the beginning.

02 · Understanding the problem

Different clients needed different routes to help.

I used research, testing and an audit of the existing experiences to understand how clients sought support and where the chatbot could fit.

Support preferences varied. Some clients wanted a quick conversation, others preferred finding an article and some wanted access to a person. Making the chatbot more prominent needed to account for those differences.

This shaped the direction: encourage clients to try the chatbot without forcing them into a single way of getting help.

03 · Design decisions

A shared direction across different platforms.

The challenge wasn’t to repeat the same screen everywhere. It was to establish a recognisable approach that could work within each platform’s layout and existing components.

I explored the direction through wireframes and prototypes, working with developers early to understand what could be implemented within the timeframe.

01

Make the chatbot easier to choose.

Give the upgraded chatbot a more prominent place in the help experience, creating a clearer opportunity for clients to try it.

02

Keep other help routes accessible.

Preserve access to existing support options so increased chatbot visibility didn’t come at the expense of clients who needed a different route.

03

Adapt without introducing new components.

Use the available components to support a consistent direction across platforms while accommodating their different layouts and constraints.

04 · Design and delivery

From design to implementation.

My involvement continued beyond the final designs.

Developers provided access to the test environment, where I reviewed incremental builds and discussed discrepancies with them as implementation progressed. This helped us resolve issues while the work was still taking shape.

Final walkthroughs covered interactions and visual details, including spacing, formatting and overflow. The goal was to carry the intended experience through to the implemented product—not simply deliver a set of screens.

05 · Outcome

More clients engaging with the chatbot.

~70%

More engaged chatbot conversations

The work contributed to approximately 70% more engaged chatbot conversations, supporting the business objective of increasing adoption.

The chatbot engine was also being upgraded, so the interface changes shouldn’t receive sole credit for the outcome. My contribution was shaping how clients discovered and accessed that capability across the platforms.

Looking back

Making the constraints part of the solution.

This project required a pragmatic approach: enough investigation to make informed decisions, enough exploration to find a workable solution and close collaboration to deliver within three weeks.

The result wasn’t a new set of components. It was a different use of the existing system to balance business ambition, client needs and implementation constraints.

Higher engagement demonstrated increased use. It did not, on its own, establish whether clients received better answers or continued choosing the chatbot over time. Those would be important questions for follow-up evaluation.
