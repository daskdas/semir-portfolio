SAXO BANK · REFER A FRIEND

From overlooked to understood

Increasing participation in Saxo Bank's referral programme was a key business priority, prompting us to revisit the existing experience. Many clients were unaware of it or struggled to find it. And for those who did discover it, unfamiliar financial terminology and unclear reward conditions made it difficult to understand how the offer worked. That uncertainty made it harder for clients to see the value in referring someone.

My role

  • Project planning and discovery
  • UX research and design validation
  • End-to-end UX/UI design across Saxo Bank's trading platforms
  • UX direction and design collaboration with the public website team

Results

  • +20% Increase in referrals
  • −50% Referral-related support inquiries
  • 3× Year-over-year referral volume** The redesign was part of a wider initiative involving product, marketing, sales and customer support.

Timeline

3 months

Platforms

Final design and solutions

Find the programme.

Clients can discover the referral programme through their portfolio, profile or Saxo Bank's public website.

Understand the offer.

A dedicated referral page explains how the programme works, what each person receives and how commission credits can be used.

Share an invitation.

Sharing options are adapted to desktop and mobile, giving clients different ways to invite their friends.

Beyond the trading platform.

The public website supports two different journeys: existing clients discovering the programme independently and prospective clients arriving through a friend's invitation.

Public website experience developed in collaboration with the web team.

Understanding the barriers to participation.

Together with the Product Owner and Project Manager, we established the scope of the project, building on existing customer research and business objectives.

Of surveyed clients were unaware of the referral programme, depending on the region.

Despite the programme already being available within the trading platforms, awareness remained limited across the surveyed regions.

1 in 4

Struggled to explain the reward.

Unfamiliar financial terminology, particularly around commission credits, and unclear reward conditions made the offer difficult to understand.

Looking beyond the interface.

Improving participation meant understanding the referral experience as a whole, rather than simply redesigning the existing interface.

I mapped the referral journey from both the sender's and recipient's perspectives, drawing on existing research, benchmarking, competitive analysis and usability testing to identify where the experience could be improved.

Friction beyond the trading platforms.

The public website introduced further complexity, requiring visitors to manually select their country before accessing the relevant referral offer.

  1. Discover the programme
  2. Select a country
  3. Access the relevant referral offer

Setting the direction.

Increase visibility.
Without disrupting the core trading experience.
Improve comprehension.
Without losing important financial and regulatory information.
Simplify participation.
While accommodating different markets and platforms.

How much space should a referral get on a trading platform?

Leadership wanted more promotional placements. I supported the goal, but clients came to the portfolio to check their investments. Putting the offer there made sense. Repeated prompts would compete with that task.

I advocated for one portfolio button and a permanent entry in the profile.

Give people an offer they can explain.

The original dialog held the conditions in a table. Before sharing, clients had to work out three things. What would I get? What would my friend get? And what could either of us do with commission credits?

Who earns what?

The page names each person first, then explains how credits cover fees on future trades.

Across 14 markets. Local rules affected which incentives Saxo could offer. The page structure accommodated those differences. I also created regional imagery and a composition guide so teams could update banners independently.

What happens when the friend opens the link?

The client shared from a platform they already used. Their friend arrived on a public website, where the link itself could raise doubts. I provided UX direction and the design foundation for that handoff. The web team developed the landing page from there.

What changed after launch?

Saxo reported more referral activity and fewer referral-related support questions.

A campaign around Saxo’s new pricing ran during the same period. Programme figures capture that wider activity, so they can’t isolate the redesign’s contribution.

Unreleased exploration · referral tracking

We designed and developed a referral-status and high-score feature. Compliance had been involved and later changed its position over GDPR-related concerns. The feature was deactivated before release and contributed to none of the reported outcomes.

The decision I’d carry forward was to ask what a client needed before putting an invitation in front of more people. Here, that meant choosing two places clients already visited and giving them an offer they could explain in their own words.
